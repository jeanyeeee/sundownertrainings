🧰 Tools & Equipment

Know your kit before the session. Everything is provided on-site — check it all 30 min before guests arrive.

🍦

Ice Cream Making

  • 📦

    Styrofoam Ice Cream Box

    The insulated star of the show! The ice cream mixture bowl sits inside the Styrofoam box packed with ice and rock salt. The insulation keeps the cold concentrated for a faster, more even freeze — no machine needed for this part.

  • 🥄

    Measuring Spoons & Cups

    For accurate ingredient portions. Getting the ratio right is critical — too much honey affects the freezing point and final texture.

  • 🍳

    Saucepan (Heavy-Based)

    For warming the cream, milk and honey, and cooking the custard base. A heavy base ensures even heat distribution — no hot spots that could scramble the eggs.

  • 🥣

    Mixing Bowls

    One large bowl for the ice bath (to cool the custard), one medium bowl for whisking egg yolks and sugar. Heat-safe glass or stainless steel recommended.

  • 🫆

    Whisk

    For ribboning the egg yolks and sugar, and for continuous stirring of the custard base over heat. A balloon whisk gives the best control and prevents lumps.

  • 🧊

    Ice & Rock Salt

    Packed around the ice cream bowl inside the Styrofoam box. Rock salt lowers the freezing point of ice dramatically — down to around -10°C — enabling the mixture to freeze without a machine.

  • ⚙️

    Cuisinart Compressor Machine

    Our ice cream churner. Ready to use at any time — no pre-freeze needed. Pour in the cooled custard base and let it churn to a perfect soft-serve consistency in ~20–25 minutes.

  • 🍦

    Ice Cream Scoop

    For the satisfying final scoop into bowls. Run under warm water before scooping for a clean, rounded presentation.

  • 🥣

    Serving Bowls

    Bowls only for serving — allows guests to pile on toppings generously without worrying about balance. Have them pre-arranged at the toppings station looking inviting.

  • 🍬

    Baking Tray (Lined)

    For pouring and setting the honeycomb. Line with baking paper — do not grease. The honeycomb should peel off cleanly once fully cooled and set.

  • 🫙

    Freezer-Safe Tub with Lid

    For transferring the churned ice cream to set overnight. Label with the date. Have one pre-labelled tub ready per batch.

  • 🧻

    Towels & Cleanup Supplies

    Ice cream making is deliciously messy! Keep paper towels, a damp cloth, and a small bin within reach throughout the session.

🐝

Bee Encounter

  • 🥼

    Bee Suits (1 per guest + facilitator)

    Full-body white suits with integrated veil. White is calming to bees. Check all zips — front, cuffs, and veil — are fully sealed before approaching the hive. Have a range of sizes ready.

  • 🧤

    Beekeeping Gloves + Powder

    Long-gauntlet gloves that overlap the suit sleeves for a full seal. Dust with powder before putting on — this helps them slide on easily and ensures a bee-proof fit at the wrists. Size up if unsure.

  • 💨

    Bee Smoker

    Loaded and lit with wet leaves for cool, gentle smoke. The wet leaves slow the burn and produce a thick, cooler smoke that calms bees without heat stress. Always test the smoke temperature on your wrist before use — it should feel cool.

  • 🍃

    Wet Leaves (Smoker Fuel)

    Collected fresh before the session. Soak in water for 10–15 minutes for optimal moisture level. Dry leaves burn too hot and too fast — wet leaves give a slow, cool, steady smoke ideal for a calm hive visit.

  • 📋

    Bee Board (Educational Display)

    Set up near the Apiary before the session. Should include: bee species comparison, lifecycle diagram, hive anatomy, and fun facts. Acts as a reference point during the theory section and while guests observe the hive.

  • 🪵

    Hive Tool (J-Tool / Hive Opener)

    Used to gently pry apart frames sealed by propolis (bee glue). Essential for opening the hive without damaging comb or disturbing the colony more than needed. Keep it clean between sessions.

Do separate equipment checks for each phase: run through the ice cream kit first (30 min before), then do a dedicated bee encounter kit check 15 min before heading to the Apiary — suits fitted, smoker lit and tested, Bee Board in position.

Know Your Frozen Treats: A Delicious History

⏱ 5 min ▼

Ice cream didn't just appear in a tub at the supermarket — it took centuries of travel, culture, and creativity to become what it is today. Walk guests through this delicious origin story using the map and timeline below.

🗺️ The ancient Silk Road — the highway that carried spices, silk, and the first frozen treats westward from Persia to Europe.

🍧 The Journey of Ice Cream

🕌

🇮🇷 Persia — The Origin

Persians were the first to enjoy sharbat — a mixture of snow or ice with fruit syrups, rose water, and grape juice. This was a royal treat served to the ruling class as far back as 400 BCE. The word "sorbet" comes directly from the Persian sharbat.

🛤️

🛣️ The Silk Road — The Highway

Traders along the Silk Road carried not just spices and silk, but ideas — including the concept of iced drinks and frozen treats. As the recipe travelled westward, each culture added its own twist. Arab traders introduced sharbat to the Mediterranean world, where it became sorbetto in Italian.

🥐

🇫🇷 France — Milk Enters the Picture

When the frozen iced treat arrived in France in the 17th century, French cooks added a brilliant twist — milk. This created a creamier, more indulgent version. King Louis XIV was reportedly a fan, and frozen desserts became a centrepiece of aristocratic banquets. The French called it fromage glacé (frozen cream).

🍕

🇮🇹 Italy — Heavy Cream & Gelato

Italian cooks — particularly in Florence — added heavy cream and egg yolks to the mix, producing a richer, denser frozen dessert: gelato. The Medici family is often credited with spreading gelato across Europe. Italian chefs brought the recipe to France, which then inspired what we know today as modern ice cream.

🌍 Origin Era What They Added Result
🇮🇷 Persia ~400 BCE Ice + fruit syrup + rosewater Sharbat / Sorbet
🇫🇷 France 17th Century + Milk Fromage Glacé
🇮🇹 Italy 17th–18th Century + Heavy Cream + Egg Yolks Gelato → Ice Cream

A Little History: How Ice Cream is Made

⏱ 8 min ▼

Before the freezer was invented, making ice cream was a labour of love. Walk guests through the original hand-made method — which is almost exactly what they'll be doing today — before introducing the modern compressor machine.

The Original Method — No Machine Needed! 🧊

This method dates back to at least the 17th century and remained popular well into the 19th century until mechanical refrigeration became common. Sound familiar? It's exactly what you'll be doing today with the mason jars! 🫙

1

Mix the Base

A sweetened mixture of cream, milk, and flavourings (like vanilla or fruit) was hand-mixed in a metal canister.

2

Ice & Salt Bath

The metal canister was surrounded by ice mixed with salt. The salt lowered the freezing point of the ice, creating a temperature cold enough to freeze cream — down to around -10°C!

3

Churn by Hand

The canister was turned or shaken — often using a crank freezer — to freeze the mixture evenly and prevent large ice crystals from forming. This is why agitation = creaminess!

4

Pack & Harden

Once thickened, the ice cream was packed and left to harden in more ice before serving. The whole process took 30–60 minutes of arm work!

⚙️ Modern Compressor Machine

We use a Cuisinart Compressor Ice Cream Maker — a low-entry pro compressor that does in 20 minutes what took your great-great-grandparents an hour of cranking!

❄️ Pre-Frozen Bowl

  • Requires 12–24 hrs pre-freeze
  • One batch only per freeze
  • Cheaper to buy
  • No motor — relies on stored cold

⚙️ Compressor Machine ✅

  • Ready anytime — no pre-freeze
  • Multiple batches back-to-back
  • Built-in cooling motor
  • Consistent, professional results

🔄 How the Compressor Works — Step by Step

🔥

① Compression

The compressor squeezes the refrigerant gas (R134a), raising its pressure and temperature. This is the "engine" of the whole cycle.

💨

② Condenser — Heat Out

The hot gas flows through condenser coils with a fan. Heat is released into the air, and the gas cools into a liquid. This is why the back of the machine feels warm!

🔩

③ Expansion Valve — Pressure Drop

The liquid refrigerant passes through a tiny expansion valve, rapidly expanding into a cold, low-pressure gas. The rapid expansion causes a dramatic temperature drop.

❄️

④ Evaporator — Freeze the Ice Cream!

The cold gas flows through coils surrounding the mixing bowl (the evaporator), drawing heat out of the ice cream mixture — freezing it from the outside in while the paddle churns.

🔁

⑤ Repeat the Cycle

The refrigerant — now warmed by absorbing heat from the ice cream — returns to the compressor, and the cycle repeats continuously until your ice cream is frozen to perfection! 🍦

Making the Honey Ice Cream Mixture

⏱ 30 min ▼

This is the heart of the experience — guests make a proper custard-based ice cream from scratch. The technique uses gentle heat and tempering to build a silky, professional base. Keep the energy calm and focused here; this step requires attention!

110g

Step-by-Step Method

1

Warm the Cream, Milk & Honey

Combine the heavy cream, whole milk and honey (170g) in a saucepan. Heat gently over medium heat — do not boil. Stir to dissolve the honey fully. The warmth unlocks honey's floral aromatics. Let guests take a big sniff at this point! 🍯

The honey should turn a deep brown colour — but not burnt! Watch the heat closely. Deep amber = perfect flavour depth. Black or acrid smell = start over. Trust your nose as much as your eyes.
2

Whisk Egg Yolks + Sugar

In a separate bowl, whisk the 9 egg yolks with the granulated sugar (50g) until pale and slightly thickened. This is called "ribboning" — the mixture should fall off the whisk in a slow ribbon. This step begins building the custard structure.

3

⚡ Temper the Cream into the Honey

Slowly ladle a little warm cream mixture into the honey pan, whisking constantly. Then gradually pour in the rest. Tempering prevents scrambling — you're gently raising the temperature of the cream base before adding the eggs. Slow and steady!

Temper the warm cream into the honey first — not the other way round. Add just a small ladle at a time, whisking as you go, to gently bring the honey up to temperature before combining fully.
4

⚡ Temper Cream + Honey into Eggs

Now pour the warm cream-honey mixture slowly into the egg yolk bowl, whisking the whole time. Then return everything to the saucepan over low-medium heat. Keep stirring continuously — bring to a gentle simmer.

Add only a little cream-honey mixture at a time into the eggs — a small ladleful first, whisk well, then another, and so on. Pouring too much too fast will cook the eggs and make scrambled eggs instead of custard. Patience here makes all the difference!
5

Cook Until Thick — The Spoon Test

Stir constantly over gentle heat until the custard thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and fall off slowly. This is called nappe consistency. Run a finger across the spoon — it should leave a clean line that holds.

⚠️ Watch the heat! If it gets too hot, the eggs will scramble. If you see lumps forming, remove from heat immediately and strain through a sieve.
Use a small flame / low heat throughout this step. Switch from a whisk to a spatula — it covers more of the pan bottom and prevents the custard from cooking and sticking underneath. Keep the spatula moving in a figure-eight pattern the entire time.
6

Whisk in Crème Fraîche

Remove from heat. Add 2 pinches of salt, then whisk in the crème fraîche (110g). This adds a beautiful slight tang that rounds out the sweetness of the honey. The mixture should be glossy and smooth.

7

Ice Bath — Cool It Down

Pour the custard into a bowl set over an ice bath (a larger bowl filled with ice and cold water). Stir occasionally until completely cooled. A cold base = better churn = creamier ice cream. Don't rush this step!

While the mixture cools in the ice bath, keep guests engaged by asking: "Why do we need to cool the mixture before churning it?" — this bridges directly into the next step about the ice cream machine.

Making the Honeycomb

⏱ 20 min ▼

While the ice cream base cools, it's time for the drama of the session — making honeycomb from scratch! This step involves high heat and a spectacular chemical reaction that guests will absolutely love to watch. Keep safety front of mind.

🍬
Caster Sugar
200g
🍯
Honey
75g
🫧
Baking Soda
2 tsp
🧪
Cream of Tartar
¼ tsp

Step-by-Step Method

1

Boil the Honey + Sugar

Combine caster sugar (200g) and honey (75g) in a heavy-based saucepan. Heat over medium-high heat without stirring until the mixture reaches a deep amber caramel colour. Swirling the pan gently is fine — but no stirring with a spoon!

🌡️ Target temperature: ~150°C (hard crack stage). The caramel should look like dark golden syrup and smell deeply toasty.
2

Drop in the Bicarb + Cream of Tartar

Remove from heat immediately. Add the cream of tartar (¼ tsp) then the baking soda (2 tsp) all at once. The mixture will explode into foam — this is the CO₂ reaction! It happens fast!

3

Stir Until Foamy

Quickly stir a few times to incorporate — just enough to distribute the foam evenly. Don't overmix or you'll deflate the bubbles! The mixture should look thick, airy, and gloriously golden.

Do NOT overmix once the bicarb goes in. A few quick folds is all it takes. Over-stirring pops the CO₂ bubbles that create the honeycomb's airy texture — you'll end up with a flat, dense slab instead of light, crunchy honeycomb.
4

Pour & Let It Set

Quickly pour onto a lined baking tray — do not spread or press! Let the honeycomb settle naturally so the air bubbles stay intact. Leave to cool and set at room temperature for 15–20 minutes. It will harden into a crisp, glassy slab.

Cut and prepare your baking paper BEFORE you start boiling the caramel. Once the caramel is ready, you'll have no time to fumble with paper. Tray should be lined, flat, and waiting on the bench before you turn on the heat.

🔬 The Science Behind the Crunch

When baking soda (sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃) meets the hot acidic caramel, it decomposes and releases carbon dioxide (CO₂) gas. The thick caramel traps these bubbles before they can escape — creating the honeycomb's signature airy, porous structure. Cream of tartar (an acid) boosts this reaction and stops the sugar re-crystallising.

Have guests stand back slightly when the bicarb goes in — the dramatic foam rise is a visual wow! Use this as a teaching moment: "This is the same reaction as a baking soda volcano — just tastier." 🌋

The Sundowner Story & Singapore's Bees

⏱ 10 min ▼

Before suiting up, gather guests around the Bee Board for the theory portion. Start with The Sundowner's story — it's personal, surprising, and immediately builds emotional connection to the bees they're about to meet.

🏡 The Sundowner Story

The Sundowner didn't set out to become a beekeeping sanctuary. One day, we discovered a massive colony of bees had moved into our space — not a few bees, but an entire thriving community. Instead of calling pest control, we made a choice: to rescue them. We called in experts, relocated the colony safely, and began learning everything we could about bees. Today, our rooftop Apiary is a testament to what happens when you choose curiosity over fear. 🐝

Singapore is home to 130 species of bees — but only 4 of those are honeybees. The other 126 are solitary bees, which don't live in hives, don't produce honey, and are rarely seen. Solitary bees are incredibly important pollinators — each one is its own tiny, industrious world!
How many bee species are in Singapore — and how many of them are honeybees?
✅ 130 species total, but only 4 are honeybees! The rest are solitary bees — no hive, no honey, but just as important for pollination.

🐝 Singapore's 3 Honeybee Species

Apis cerana
Asian / Eastern Honeybee
⭐ Our Resident Bees!

Medium-sized and incredibly adaptable. Apis cerana is the bee you'll meet at our Apiary today. They build enclosed hives — like a cozy dark hollow — and are calmer and more manageable than their giant cousins.

Apis florea
Dwarf Honeybee
🌿 Wild

The smallest honeybee in Singapore — about half the size of a Western honeybee. They build single-comb nests on exposed branches. Can be red or black. Gentle by nature and rarely sting unprovoked.

Apis dorsata
Giant Honeybee
🌿 Wild

The largest honeybee in the world — up to 3× bigger than a regular bee! Builds massive exposed combs on high branches and building overhangs. Known for their defensive nature — give them space!

Solitary bees like the Carpenter Bee and Sweat Bee don't live in hives — every female is her own queen, nest-builder, and honey-maker all at once. No teamwork required!

Life Inside the Hive — Who's Who?

⏱ 8 min ▼

A honeybee hive is one of the most sophisticated societies on earth — and it all runs on three castes. Use the gender reveal as a hook; guests are almost always surprised!

The hive has 3 types of bees: Queen, Drone, and Worker. Can you guess which ones are female?
✅ The Queen and all Workers are female! Drones are the only males — and their sole purpose is to mate with a queen. After mating, they die. In winter, the workers actually kick them out of the hive. Nature is brutal! 😅

The Lifecycle of a Bee

⏱ 5 min ▼

From a single egg to a fully-fledged bee in just 21 days — the lifecycle of a honeybee is a masterclass in biological efficiency.

🥚
Egg

Queen lays a tiny rice-grain egg in a cell. Hatches in 3 days.

→
🐛
Larvae

Fed bee bread (pollen + honey mix) by nurse bees. Grows rapidly for 6 days.

→
🫘
Pupae

Cell is capped with wax. Inside, the larva transforms — wings, legs, eyes form over 12 days.

→
🐝
Adult Bee

Bites through the wax cap, emerges, and immediately cleans its own cell — ready for the next egg!

The very first thing a newborn bee does after emerging is clean the cell it was born in — making its bed, ready for the next generation. A tiny act of housekeeping that keeps the whole colony running!
Worker bees are fed something special to become queens instead of workers — what is it?
✅ Royal Jelly! Nursing bees feed eligible larvae (less than 2 days old) an exclusive diet of royal jelly — a protein-rich secretion. This triggers completely different development: larger body, functional ovaries, and a lifespan of years instead of weeks. Same egg, totally different destiny!
🎯 Facilitator: Point to the Bee Board lifecycle diagram while telling this story. If you have a capped brood frame available, pass it around — seeing the capped cells in real life makes the pupae stage click immediately.

Worker Bee Duties — A Career in 6 Weeks

⏱ 5 min ▼

Worker bees don't have one job — they have a career progression. In their 6-week lifespan, they graduate through three distinct roles based on age, starting inside the hive and eventually venturing into the world.

① 🍼

Nursing Bee

The hive intern. Starts career inside the hive, tending to the queen and feeding larvae. Produces royal jelly and bee bread. Think of it as starting your career in training before seeing the outside world.

② 🛡️

Guard Bee

The hive's security team. Stationed at the entrance to defend against threats — bears, wasps, ants, curious humans. Guard bees use their stingers as a last resort, releasing an alarm pheromone to call for backup when threatened.

③ 🌸

Foraging Bee

The adventurer. Flies up to 5km from the hive collecting pollen (protein) and nectar (carbohydrates) — storing them in special pouches on their hind legs. Returns to perform the "waggle dance" to share directions with hive-mates!

Foraging bees carry pollen in special structures called corbiculae (pollen baskets) on their hind legs. You can actually see little orange or yellow pollen packs on bees as they fly — look for it next time you're in the garden!
A foraging bee collects two types of food for the hive. What are they, and what do they provide nutritionally?
✅ Pollen = protein (used to make bee bread, which feeds larvae) and Nectar = carbohydrates (converted into honey, which is the hive's energy reserve). Bees are incredibly efficient nutritionists!
🎯 Facilitator: Ask guests: "Which bee role sounds most like your job?" — it's a great icebreaker that gets laughter and keeps energy up before suiting up.

Suit Up & Photo Time!

⏱ 10 min ▼

This is a highlight moment — guests suiting up always generates excitement, laughter, and great photos. Set it up as a fun ritual, not just a safety step!

🥼

Bee Suit

Full-body white suits. Check zips are fully closed — especially wrists and ankles. White is calming to bees!

🧤

Gloves + Powder

Dusted with powder to help them slide on easily and create a bee-proof seal at the wrists.

💨

Smoker

Lit with wet leaves (cooler, gentler smoke). Ready to use at the hive — never point directly at bees.

📋

Bee Board

Educational display positioned near the Apiary — visible during the hive visit for quick reference.

  • ✅ Step into suit legs first, then arms
  • ✅ Zip up the full suit — front, cuffs, and veil zipper
  • ✅ Tuck trouser cuffs into socks if wearing open shoes
  • ✅ Pull on gloves and press sleeve over glove cuff to seal

At the Apiary — Reading the Hive

⏱ 10 min ▼

At the Apiary, help guests understand the architecture of the hive before opening it. The hive is a marvel of natural engineering — each zone has a purpose.

🍯

Top Box — Honey Stores

Honey is stored at the top because it's the heaviest. Bees pack capped honeycomb here — the golden reward of all their foraging work. This is the section beekeepers harvest from.

🐣

Middle Box — Brood Zone & Bee Bread

The nursery. This is where the queen lays her eggs, larvae develop, and bee bread (pollen + honey) is stored to feed the growing larvae. Warm, protected, and the beating heart of the colony.

🥜

Bottom — Queen Cells

Queen cells are built at the bottom edges of frames — they look like peanut shells hanging down! These special cells are where new queens are raised, fed exclusively on royal jelly until they're ready to hatch.

Where in the wild do different bee species prefer to nest?
✅ Asian bees (Apis cerana): Love dark, enclosed spaces — like a tree hollow, wall cavity, or even Winnie the Pooh's honey pot! 🍯 Giant & Dwarf bees (Apis dorsata & florea): Build exposed single combs hanging from tree branches or building overhangs — completely out in the open!
🎯 Facilitator: Before opening the hive, point to the outside of the box and ask guests to guess which zone is which based on what they've just learned. This makes them feel like experts — and they usually get it right!
💨

Smoke the Bees — Say Hello! 👋

⏱ 15 min ▼

Time to open the hive! Use the smoker to calm the bees before lifting the frames. Explain the science of smoke to guests — it's a beautifully elegant evolutionary trick.

💨 Why Smoke Works

Smoke simulates a forest fire — one of the honeybee's oldest evolutionary threats. When bees smell smoke, their instinct kicks in: they gorge on honey to prepare for potential evacuation. A full belly makes them docile, slow, and calm. They're too busy eating to worry about defending!

Smoke also masks alarm pheromones — so even if one bee gets agitated, its distress signal can't spread to the rest of the colony. We use wet leaves for cooler, gentler smoke that won't overheat or harm the bees.

🔍 What to Look For in the Hive

  • 🥚
    Eggs — tiny white grains standing upright in cells, like a grain of rice. Only visible in good light — means the queen was here within 3 days!
  • 🐛
    Larvae & Capped Brood — curled white grubs in open cells, brown-capped cells in sealed brood. Look for the different stages side by side!
  • 👑
    The Queen — larger, longer abdomen, surrounded by a circle of attentive nurses. Can you spot her? She usually moves deliberately, unlike the workers who scurry.
  • 🟡
    Bee Bread & Pollen Stores — multi-coloured packed cells of pollen next to brood — the pantry! Different colours represent different flower sources.
  • 🍯
    Honey — capped with white beeswax at the top of frames. Fully ripe honey cells will be completely sealed — ready to harvest!
Bees use different-coloured pollen from many flower species — you can sometimes see rainbow patterns of yellow, orange, red and purple pollen packed into cells side by side. It's the hive's version of a balanced diet! 🌈
🎯 Facilitator: Move slowly and deliberately at the hive. Narrate what you're seeing in real time. Let guests try puffing the smoker — it gives them agency and makes the moment feel real. End by letting guests hold a frame (if safe) for the ultimate photo moment! 📸

Scoop, Top & Taste Your Creation!

⏱ 15 min ▼

Back from the Apiary — it's time for the big payoff! The pre-made honey ice cream is waiting. Scoop it into cones or bowls and head to the toppings station — honeycomb you made, edible flowers, and a dramatic honey drizzle. 🍯

  • Break the honeycomb slab into craggy shards for topping
  • Encourage guests to photograph their creation first
  • Do a group "first taste" toast — everyone eats at the same time!
  • Ask: "Can you taste the floral notes from the local honey?"
Have extra honey on hand for a dramatic drizzle — this is THE photo moment. The combination of the honeycomb you made + the ice cream you made + honey from the hive you just visited = a full circle moment guests will never forget!
